# Style Preset — Cinematic Blockbuster

**Default preset.** Hollywood live-action realism — what a $200M theatrical trailer looks like.

## When this preset applies

- `Adaptation style` answer is "live-action film adaptation" or any phrasing that implies real-world cinematography (theatrical trailer, blockbuster, live-action movie, prestige drama).
- Default fallback when the user's adaptation style is unclear.

## Render style line

`photorealistic 3D cinematic render, modern live-action {{GENRE}} blockbuster style, ARRI Alexa cinematography, anamorphic lens character with shallow depth of field, realistic PBR textures, volumetric lighting, atmospheric haze`

## Lens / camera language

- Anamorphic feel — 1.85:1 to 2.39:1 framing intent, oval bokeh, subtle horizontal lens flares (sparingly).
- Wide-to-mid focal lengths (24mm, 35mm, 50mm) for hero work; 85mm+ for portrait beats.
- Shallow depth of field on character close-ups; deep focus for environmental scale.
- Lens artifacts where they sell realism: subtle chromatic aberration on bright highlights, light volumetrics, dust mote sparkle in shafts.

## Material / lighting language

- Physically-based rendering (PBR). Realistic skin subsurface scattering, fabric weave, metal microsurface, weathered wood and stone.
- Practical-feeling light sources — sun, fire, neon, sodium streetlamp, flashlight, monitor glow. No magical fill light.
- Volumetric haze, dust, smoke, sparks, weather, god rays as appropriate.
- Sweat, grime, blood, soot, scratches — surfaces tell stories.

## Color grade defaults

- Deep contrast with crushed shadows and protected highlights (modern theatrical look).
- Hero/threat color separation — pick a hero color and a threat color from `{{SCOPE}}`'s palette and let them recur.
- Teal-and-orange is overused; prefer scope-specific palettes (e.g., Halo: green/copper/violet plasma; Bloodborne: bone/ochre/crimson; Zelda: gold/jade/twilight purple).
- Slight desaturation in horror beats; saturation lifts in triumphant climax.

## What to avoid

- **No cel-shading, no illustration, no painterly look, no video-game-screenshot styling.**
- No flat anime lighting.
- No theatrical anamorphic flares packed into every shot — they should be rare and earned.
- No HDR-overcooked highlights or lifted shadows that read as digital instead of cinematic.
- No "smooth digital" face plastic — preserve pore-level skin detail.

## Reference director / studio anchors

Recommend 2–3 from this list based on the trailer's genre tilt:

- **Scale and silence:** Denis Villeneuve (Blade Runner 2049, Dune)
- **Industrial dread:** Ridley Scott (Alien)
- **Military tension:** James Cameron (Aliens)
- **Practical-feeling spectacle:** Christopher Nolan (Interstellar, Tenet)
- **Human-scale awe:** Gareth Edwards (Rogue One, The Creator)
- **Adventure earnestness:** Steven Spielberg (Raiders, Jurassic Park)
- **Symmetry and whimsy:** Wes Anderson (for the rare comedy-leaning trailer)
- **Fairytale horror:** Guillermo del Toro (Pan's Labyrinth, Crimson Peak)
- **Monumental architecture and stillness:** Stanley Kubrick (2001)
