# Style Preset — Pre-Rendered Cutscene

High-fidelity CG cinematic — the in-house cinematic team treatment (Blizzard cinematics, Square Enix CG, Riot in-house, Bungie cinematics).

## When this preset applies

- `Adaptation style` answer is "game cinematic", "pre-rendered cutscene", "CG trailer", or "Blizzard-style cinematic".
- Trailers meant to look like the cinematic intro to a game, not gameplay and not a live-action film.
- The middle ground between live-action realism and stylized animation — photoreal materials and lighting, but with slightly idealized character proportions and faces (game-character-on-the-box-art look).

## Render style line

`high-fidelity pre-rendered CG cinematic, AAA in-house cinematic team aesthetic (Blizzard / Square Enix / Bungie quality), photoreal materials with slightly idealized character proportions, ray-traced global illumination, sub-surface scattering on skin, anamorphic lens character with cinematic shallow depth of field`

## Lens / camera language

- Anamorphic feel (matches live-action cinematic conventions) — oval bokeh, subtle lens flares.
- Wide focal lengths (24-35mm) for environmental scale moments; 50-85mm for hero portraits and action.
- Cinematic shallow depth of field on character work.
- Camera moves are smoother than gameplay (full virtual Steadicam, crane, dolly) but punchier than live-action — CG cinematics often use more aggressive camera moves than live-action because there's no physical rig limit.
- Leaning into "impossible camera" moments: through walls, inside engines, beside a bullet in flight — used sparingly, but available.

## Material / lighting language

- Top-tier PBR. Skin sub-surface scattering with idealized but readable detail (not photoreal pore-level — slightly cleaner).
- Cloth and armor at hero-prop fidelity: every weave, scratch, and edge wear visible.
- Hair groom rendered at film quality — per-strand, not card-based.
- Volumetric lighting heavily used — god rays, atmospheric haze, lit smoke columns.
- Practical-feeling light sources, but with permission to be slightly more dramatic than a live-action set could rig in practice.

## Color grade defaults

- Theatrical-grade contrast (deeper than gameplay, similar to blockbuster).
- Stronger hero/threat color separation than gameplay preset — these cinematics are short and need to land emotional beats fast.
- Studio palette signatures: Blizzard cinematics tend toward deep saturated palettes with strong key/rim separation; Square Enix tends toward cooler, more muted; Bungie tends toward strong hero color (orange/blue Halo signature) over neutral environments.

## What to avoid

- **Avoid live-action plastic-photoreal face syndrome** — CG cinematics maintain slight character idealization (heroic jawlines, chosen-one eyes). They should look like the cover of the game box, not like an actor.
- **No gameplay HUD elements, no in-engine UI overlays.**
- **No temporal AA ghosting, no screen-space reflection holes** — these cinematics are pre-rendered with film-quality samples, not real-time approximations.
- **No handheld jitter language** — cinematic teams use stabilized virtual cameras almost exclusively.
- **No anime / cel-shading drift** — this preset is photoreal, not stylized.

## Reference director / studio anchors

In-house cinematic teams known for this aesthetic:

- **Blizzard Cinematics** (Overwatch, Diablo, Warcraft, StarCraft cinematics) — the gold standard for CG cinematic storytelling, strong hero/villain framing.
- **Square Enix Visual Works** (Final Fantasy CG cinematics, Kingsglaive) — high-character-fidelity, dramatic lighting, slow-build action.
- **Bungie Cinematics** (Halo, Destiny intro/launch trailers) — sci-fi spectacle, environmental scale, hero-suit detail.
- **Riot Games Cinematics** (Arcane spinoffs aside; the live-action-feel CG trailers like K/DA, Awaken) — character-driven, music-led.
- **Plastic Wax / Digic Pictures** (Assassin's Creed cinematics, Halo Wars cinematic) — outsourced cinematic teams that hit similar fidelity.
- **Goodbye Kansas** (Cyberpunk 2077, Last of Us cinematics) — gritty CG with live-action sensibility.
